# Visual language

Inspired by editorial design, paper collage, stop motion, magazine layout,
premium documentary and museum exhibitions. Think Apple, Thmanyah, a Netflix
documentary title sequence, a museum wall label. Never a TikTok template.

Handmade, but finished. Every piece of tape is placed on purpose.

## The signature: how people know it is Maryam in two seconds

Four things appear in every episode of every show. Together they are the
brand. Do not ship an episode missing one.

1. **The paper tear (audio + image).** Frame 1: a single sharp paper-rip
   sound, about half a second, as the first image tears open. Always the same
   sound file. This is the audio logo. People will recognise it with their
   eyes closed before they recognise the face.
2. **The Maryam cutout.** Maryam appears at least once as a die-cut photo with
   a thick off-white paper border and a soft drop shadow, placed into the
   collage like a sticker. She is part of the page, not a talking head pasted
   on top.
3. **The paper.** Every frame sits on textured paper: warm off-white or the
   show's paper. Visible fibres, light grain. Never flat digital colour.
4. **The stamp.** The episode ends with the show's rubber stamp thudding onto
   the paper, slightly rotated, with a dull "thunk". Same stamp, same sound,
   every episode.

One brand base for everything, one world per show.

| ink | `#161412` | type, outlines |
| majd red | `#C8402B` | the brand accent: stamp, the one key word, the red thread |

One accent per frame. Majd red is for the single most important word or
object on screen, never decoration.

## Type

- **Arabic headlines:** a strong editorial Arabic face. Free starting point:
  IBM Plex Sans Arabic (Bold) for clean headlines, Amiri or Noto Naskh Arabic
  for documentary quotes and archive labels. Upgrade path: license one
  premium Arabic foundry family and use it everywhere. Check the license
  covers video and social use before using any font.
- **Typewriter:** IBM Plex Mono for dates, sources, coordinates and
  "case file" numbers. Typed on letter by letter with a soft key sound.
- **Never:** Cairo, Tajawal or any face that every template uses as default;
  outlined bouncing text; rainbow captions; more than two families on screen.
- Headlines are set like a magazine: big, tight, deliberately placed, sometimes
  cut out of paper. Body captions are small and calm.

## Motion

Everything moves. No static frame longer than one beat.

- **Collage elements** move on twos or threes (12 or 8 fps) for the
  handmade stop-motion feel. Camera moves and Maryam's footage stay at full
  frame rate. The contrast is the look.
- **Vocabulary:** paper rip, tape slapping on, cutouts sliding in with a tiny
  wobble, ink bleeding into a word, typewriter type-on, map pins dropping,
  blueprint lines drawing themselves, parallax between 3 to 5 paper layers,
  magazine page turns, a polaroid developing.
- **Film grain** on everything, subtle. Light paper-texture overlay at all
  times.
- **Transitions** are physical: tear, fold, slide, page turn. Never digital
  wipes, glitches, zoom-blurs or whooshes from a pack.
- **Camera:** slow push-ins on the collage, as if leaning over a table.

## Sound

- The paper-rip open and the stamp close are fixed (see signature).
- Foley over music: paper, tape, pen, typewriter, a mug on a desk.
- Music sits under the voice, never over it. Original or properly licensed,
  never a trending audio that dates the episode in a month.

## Frame and safe zone

- 1080x1920, 9:16. Keep text out of the top 230 px, the bottom 480 px and
  the right 230 px, where the Instagram interface sits.
- Design every frame so the central 3:4 area reads alone, because that is
  what the profile grid shows.

## Quick reject list

Reject any frame or edit that has: stock "subscribe" animations, emoji rain,
template captions, green-screen over a news photo, a logo intro, neon, chrome
3D text, or anything that looks like it came from a preset.
