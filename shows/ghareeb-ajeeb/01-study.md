**What this tells me:**

1. **The DNA is already there.** Every episode starts with a "ليش" question about
   something *everyone does or feels*. That's a real format, not a
   one-off, and it's worth keeping.
2. **It performs at the account median, not above it.** Most reels on this account
   land between roughly 100 and 450 likes. غريب عجيب sits inside that band.
   The format, as it was, doesn't break out.
3. **The best one is the cultural one.** "ليش نربط العود بالماضي" (440) beat
   the body-science ones. Culture and identity beat trivia.
4. **The comment driver is the folk-belief vs science frame.** The static-shock
   and ear-ringing episodes ("يقولون أحد يذكرك… بس العلم يقول شي ثاني") pulled the
   most comments. People love defending or correcting what their grandmother said.
5. **The cadence is irregular:** six episodes in 15 months, with gaps of up to 9
   months. The audience can't form a habit around a show that shows up by surprise.

## 5. The audience, inferred

- **Core:** UAE and Gulf, Arabic-first, reading Emirati/Khaleeji dialect
  comfortably. A large Levant and wider-Arab layer arrives through emotional
  and identity content (the Syria and Arab Reading Challenge outliers).
- **Mindset when they find you:** scrolling for something that feels good or
  useful, and responding to national pride, family, faith, and "this is me".
- **What they share:** things they can send *to someone* ("طرشووه لأهل العين",
  "أرسلوا الفيديو للشخص اللي…"). Sends are the currency.
- **What they comment on:** opinions they already hold (mixed gym, folk beliefs,
  work stress), and keywords for something useful.
- **Missing data that would change the plan:** age split, gender split,
  country split, and above all **sends per reach** and **3-second hold**. Get
  the Insights export (see section 7).

## 6. References and creators: what I could and couldn't do

You asked me to study your references and the creators who inspire you. **You
haven't sent them yet**, so I didn't pretend to. Below are the *principles* I
extracted from the established short- and long-form documentary craft that a
show like this sits next to. These are frameworks, not looks to copy. When you
send your actual list, I'll replace this section with an analysis of those
specific creators and flag every place the Bible should change.

| Craft tradition | Principle worth taking | What we must NOT take |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Map-and-desk explainer journalism (the reporter-at-a-table, paper-on-desk style) | The physical desk as a stage. Real objects make abstract stories tangible. The host's hands are a character. | Their maps, their pins-and-string look, their voice. |
| Long-form Arabic comedic explainers (the الدحيح tradition) | Arabic audiences *will* sit through dense science if the narrator is a personality and the jokes are local. Dialect over فصحى. | Comedy-first tone, long runtime, studio set. |
| Animated science channels | One question per piece. Layered reveal. Consistent visual universe so every frame is recognizable when muted. | Vector illustration, birds, bright flat colors. |
| Prestige streaming docuseries | The cold open. Withholding. Music that drops out before the reveal. A title card that becomes a ritual. | Length, pace. |
| Investigative podcasts | "The crack": the detail that doesn't fit and makes you need the answer. Narrator as fellow investigator, not authority. | Audio-only habits (long pauses that die on video). |
| Editorial magazine design | Grid, hierarchy, captions as content, the credit line. | Fashion-magazine glamour. |

**What I'm adding that none of them have:** Maryam is an **architect**. No
Arabic documentary creator I know of tells stories with an architect's
drawing language: plans, sections, elevations, dimension lines, title blocks.
That's the moat. It runs through the whole Bible.
